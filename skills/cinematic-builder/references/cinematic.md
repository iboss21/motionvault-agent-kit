# Shot design that changes the result

- A cinematic opening should stand as a strong photograph/CG artwork with text hidden. Give the subject a concrete material and light source, then reserve a calm copy area. Check portrait framing before implementation.
- Product sequence: human/context→isolated product→new angle→material macro→specification/action. Use coherent separate original assets and anchor typography where appropriate.
- Material reveal: a real second image appears under an evolving mask; edge glow/smoke are separate treatments. Brightening the original image does not reveal another subject.
- Portrait sequence: believable performance, reflective material changes, and anchored HUD can be sufficient when restraint matches the brief. Do not impose aggressive transitions on every subject.
- World sequence: author complementary environments and a purposeful boundary between them. One hero still is one scene, not a complete voyage.
- Ambient looping, grain, particles, and pointer drift support a narrative; they do not alone satisfy a scroll-story request.
- Use readable sizes in the actual containing frame. Avoid scaling essential phone navigation entirely from container-width units. Check all interaction targets and the longest custom title.
- Live HTML/WebGL and a browser-recorded Canvas interpretation may differ. Preview the exact renderer being exported and describe differences truthfully.
